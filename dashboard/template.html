F1 Dominance

Who ruled each era of Formula 1, 1950-2026

· · ·

This page explores who dominated Formula 1, season by season, from the first World Championship in 1950 to today. Dominance is measured as the percentage of races won each season, so that different eras can be compared fairly, and it is seen from three angles: teams (each constructor as officially entered), engines (the manufacturer behind the winning car, often supplying several teams at once) and factories (teams grouped by lineage: a factory that changed name and owner over the years, like Tyrrell → BAR → Honda → Brawn → Mercedes, counts as one).

How to use it

  • Slider: smooth the lines from 1 to 10 years. Short windows show every season; long ones reveal the big eras.
  • Click a name in the legend: hide or show it.
  • Double-click a name: isolate it. Double-click again to bring them all back.
  • Drag over a chart: zoom into a period. Double-click to reset.
  • Hover a line: see the exact value.

Tip: move the slider slowly and watch the peaks melt.

Overall

Dominance in F1 comes in waves: every era has eventually ended, and Ferrari is the only team that has returned to the top in very different periods. Team names alone hide part of the story, because the engine and the factory behind a car matter as much as the badge on it. And what we see depends on how we look: the smoothing window and the definition of a "team" both change the picture.

What it shows

Six eras stand out: Alfa Romeo, Ferrari and Mercedes in the 1950s; Cooper, Lotus and Tyrrell from the late 1950s to the mid-1970s; a balanced decade from 1975 to 1985 with no clear leader; McLaren and Williams taking turns from 1985 to 2000; Ferrari with Michael Schumacher in the 2000s; and Red Bull and Mercedes since 2010. The most crushing spell is Mercedes in 2014-2016, winning over 85% of races on a three-year average.

What it shows

Engines tell a different story, because one engine can win with several teams at once. The Ford Cosworth, sold to almost any team, powered one of the longest eras of dominance in F1, from the late 1960s to the early 1980s, with close to 90% of wins at its peak. Renault had two great eras with different teams (Williams and Benetton in the 1990s, Red Bull in 2010-2013). And what looks like a change at the top in 2025-2026 is continuity: McLaren and Mercedes both race with Mercedes engines.

What it shows

Grouping teams by lineage barely changes the eras, but it changes the stories. One factory, Tyrrell → Mercedes, owns two great eras about 40 years apart, with Brawn's 2009 title in between. And the name "mercedes" splits into the two different teams it really was: the 1950s works team and the current one.
